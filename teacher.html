Setting up the room

  • Open index.html in Chrome or Edge, or use the shared link. Laptops are best; tablets work with the on-screen sticks.
  • On the hub, rename Pilot 1 and Pilot 2 to your students' names. Click a pilot card before they play so stars and XP go to the right person. Progress is saved in that browser only.
  • Turn the sound on (speaker icon). The stall horn, motor hum and "correct" chimes help a lot.
  • With two students and one computer: one flies, the other is "mission control" reading the right-hand panel aloud. Swap every mission.

04Theory refresher for you

Short and plain, so you can explain it in your own words. Each part names the game moment that shows it.

Forces and the four forces (Week 1)

A force is a vector: it has a size and a direction. On an aircraft there are four: lift at 90° to the airflow, weight straight down (W = m × g), thrust along the engine, drag against the motion. When they balance (L = W and T = D), nothing accelerates, so the aircraft flies steady and level. That is Newton's 1st law. When they don't balance, F = m × a tells you which way it accelerates.

Game moment: Sky School "Four Forces". Use the Side view camera so the arrows are easy to see. The panel says "L ≈ W · T ≈ D" in green when they have it.

The lift equation and stall (Week 2)

L = ½ ρ v² S C_L. Air density ρ (thinner at Entebbe, 1.09 instead of 1.225), speed squared (double the speed → four times the lift), wing area S, and the lift coefficient C_L, which grows with angle of attack α, about 0.11 per degree. Past about 14° the air peels off the top of the wing: that is the stall. Lift drops, drag soars. You recover by lowering the nose (less α), not by pulling harder. Take-off speed comes from setting L = W: v = √(2W ÷ ρSC_Lmax) ≈ 28 m/s for the Cessna at Entebbe.

Game moment: the C_L graph in Sky School puts a pink dot on the curve where the wing is right now. In the Stall Lab they watch it go over the top.

Moments, CG and control (Week 3)

A moment is a turning effect: force × distance. Balanced when clockwise = anticlockwise. The CG is the average position of all the mass: x_CG = Σ(m·x) ÷ Σm. For stability the CG must be ahead of the neutral point; the gap is the static margin (aim for 5–15% of chord). Ailerons roll, the elevator pitches, the rudder yaws. Navigation lights: red on the left (port), green on the right (starboard), white on the tail.

Game moment: the Wind Tunnel graph shows a stable aircraft wobble back to level after a gust, and an unstable one flip over.

Propulsion and quadcopters (Week 4)

Propellers and engines push air or gas backwards, so the aircraft is pushed forwards (Newton's 3rd law). A quadcopter has no control surfaces. It steers only by changing motor speeds: all faster = climb; rear faster = pitch forward; left faster = roll right; one diagonal pair faster = yaw. Motors 1 and 3 spin clockwise and 2 and 4 anticlockwise, so their twisting reactions cancel. T/W = total thrust ÷ weight; aim for 2 or more. Motor rpm (no load) = KV × volts; a 3S LiPo is 3 × 3.7 = 11.1 V. Flight time ≈ usable Ah ÷ average A × 60.

Game moment: the motor panel in Quad Pilot is a top view of the drone. Point at the bars while they fly: "See? Rear motors faster, nose down."

Stress and materials (Week 5)

Stress = force ÷ area. Bending makes much bigger stress than pulling: for a rectangular arm σ = 6FL ÷ (b h²), so thickness h counts twice. Factor of safety = strength ÷ stress; aim for 2 or more for small drones (aircraft use 1.5 on ultimate load). Triangles are rigid, squares are not. Specific strength = strength ÷ density is how engineers compare materials. 3D prints are weakest between layers, so print arms flat.

Avionics and PID (Week 6)

Closed loop: sense → think → act, many times a second. A PID controller adds three corrections to the error e: P = Kp × e (push towards the target), D = −Kd × speed (brake before overshooting), I = Ki × the error added up over time (removes steady offsets). Too much P bounces; D calms it; I fixes a heavy battery. GPS turns signal travel time into distance (d = c × t). Three distances fix a position on a map; a fourth satellite corrects the receiver's clock.

Rockets and orbits (Week 7)

Rockets carry their own oxidiser, so they work in space. Lift-off needs T/W > 1. The rocket equation Δv = vₑ × ln(m₀ ÷ m_f) has a logarithm, so more fuel gives less and less extra speed. Dropping empty stages beats that. Orbit is falling sideways fast enough: v = √(GM ÷ r) = 7.67 km/s at 400 km (92-minute lap), 3.07 km/s at 35,786 km (one lap per day: geostationary). Launching east from the Equator (Uganda!) gives a free 465 m/s. PearlAfricaSat-1, Uganda's first satellite, was a 1U CubeSat (10 × 10 × ~11 cm) deployed from the ISS in 2023.

05Week by week, with answers

For each week: what to play, what to ask while they play, and the answers to the Quick check and Engineering challenge printed on the handout.

WEEK 1 · SAT 10 OCT · FOUNDATIONS OF FLIGHT

Forces, vectors and aircraft types

Play: Sky School · Four Forces, Land at Entebbe · Test: Week 1

Ask while they play

  • "You are climbing. Which arrow is winning: green or orange?"
  • "Speed keeps rising. Thrust or drag: which is bigger?"
  • "Read me the weight in kN. Where does that number come from?" (1,100 kg × 9.81)

Quick check answers

  1. L = W and T = D (equilibrium).
  2. Perpendicular (90°) to the relative airflow.
  3. 250 ÷ 1.852 ≈ 135 knots.
  4. Dimensions drive the geometry: change a number and the model updates.

Python lab check

Cessna 172 (1,100 kg): weight 10,791 N. 100 km/h = 54 knots = 27.8 m/s. A320 (70,000 kg): 686,700 N. 270 km/h = 145.8 knots = 75 m/s. Reflection: for medicine to an island on Lake Victoria, a hybrid VTOL or multirotor is best, because there is no runway on the island.

WEEK 2 · SAT 17 OCT · AERODYNAMICS & WING DESIGN

The lift equation, airfoils, stall

Play: Sky School · Take-off at Entebbe, Stall Lab · Try the Air: Entebbe / sea level button · Test: Week 2

Ask while they play

  • "Switch the air to sea level. Does take-off speed go up or down? Why?"
  • "Where is the pink dot on the C_L graph now? What happens past 14°?"
  • "In the stall, should you pull up harder? Try it." (It gets worse.)

Quick check answers

  1. L = ½ ρ v² S C_L: density, airspeed, wing area, lift coefficient.
  2. It becomes 4 times bigger (v²).
  3. 2% max camber, at 40% chord, 12% max thickness.
  4. Air is less dense, so more speed is needed for the same lift, so a longer runway.

Python lab check

Sea level: v = √(2 × 10,800 ÷ (1.225 × 16.2 × 1.6)) = √680 ≈ 26.1 m/s (94 km/h). Entebbe (ρ = 1.09): √765 ≈ 27.7 m/s (100 km/h). The game shows 27.6 m/s because it uses W = 1,100 × 9.81 = 10,791 N.

WEEK 3 · SAT 24 OCT · STABILITY & CONTROL

Moments, CG, control surfaces, nav lights

Play: Stability Hangar (all 5) and Sky School · Sky Rings · Test: Week 3

Ask while they play

  • "Before you slide the battery, predict where it must go. Use the table."
  • "In the wind tunnel, what happens with the CG behind the green NP?"
  • "At night you see red on YOUR right and green on YOUR left. Which way is it flying?" (towards you)

Quick check answers

  1. In equilibrium, clockwise moments = anticlockwise moments.
  2. Ailerons.
  3. The aircraft is unstable in pitch: a disturbance grows.
  4. Green.

Engineering challenge

CG = (250 × 0 + 180 × 20 + 60 × 70) ÷ 490 = 7,800 ÷ 490 ≈ 15.9 mm forward. For CG = 0: 180 × x + 60 × 70 = 0 → x = −4,200 ÷ 180 ≈ −23.3 mm (23 mm behind the centre). This is exactly the "Drone CG" mission. LED resistor: (5 − 2) ÷ 0.015 = 200 Ω → use 220 Ω.

WEEK 4 · SAT 31 OCT · PROPULSION & MULTIROTORS

Thrust, T/W, KV, LiPo, quadcopter mixing

Play: Quad Pilot (all 5) · Test: Week 4

Ask while they play

  • "What throttle did you need to hover? How does that compare with 780 ÷ 1,600?"
  • "With the medicine box, why does it feel heavy?" (T/W fell from 2.05 to 1.48)
  • "In the design review, why can't the 2212 motors go on the 250 frame?" (10-inch props hit each other)

Quick check answers

  1. So their reaction torques cancel and the drone does not spin.
  2. Speed up one diagonal pair and slow the other.
  3. 3 cells in series: 3 × 3.7 = 11.1 V.
  4. 2,000 × 11.1 = 22,200 rpm.

Engineering challenge

All-up mass = 450 + 180 + 150 = 780 g. Thrust = 4 × 400 = 1,600 g. T/W = 1,600 ÷ 780 ≈ 2.05: it just passes, with almost no margin. Two improvements: a lighter or smaller battery, stronger motors/bigger props, a lighter (carbon) frame, or a lighter camera. Worked example from the handout: 600 g drone, 4 × 450 g → T/W 3; 1.2 Ah at 12 A → 6 minutes.

WEEK 5 · SAT 7 NOV · STRUCTURES, MATERIALS & MANUFACTURING

Stress, factor of safety, trusses, 3D printing

Play: Strong & Light · Test: Week 5

Ask while they play

  • "The PLA 10 × 4 arm: direct stress is 0.15 MPa but bending stress is 28 MPa. Why so different?"
  • "Double the thickness: what happens to stress?" (¼)
  • "Why does the balsa block look so big if it weighs the same?"

Quick check answers

  1. σ = F ÷ A, in Pa or N/mm² (MPa).
  2. High strength and stiffness for its low density (specific strength).
  3. Prints are weakest between layers; flat keeps layers along the arm.
  4. The roll angle (tilt), from how gravity splits between the y and z axes.
WEEK 6 · SAT 14 NOV · AVIONICS SYSTEMS & AUTOPILOTS

Instruments, GPS, fly-by-wire, PID

Play: Autopilot Lab · Test: Week 6

Ask while they play

  • "Kp = 2, Kd = 0: describe the graph." (endless bounce)
  • "What did Kd do to the bounce?"
  • "The heavy battery stops 16 cm low. Which term fixes that?" (I)

Quick check answers

  1. Total (pitot) pressure and static pressure.
  2. Three for position, plus one to correct the receiver's clock error.
  3. It damps the motion: reacts to rate of change and reduces overshoot.
  4. The pilot's stick sends requests to computers, which move the control surfaces.

Engineering challenge

Kp = 6, Kd = 3.5 reaches 9.5 m at 1.7 s with 2.4% overshoot, settled in 2.6 s. Kp = 5, Kd = 3 also passes (3.9%). The handout's Kp = 2, Kd = 1.5 overshoots 12.8%, so it fails the 5% target. With the 10% heavier battery, Ki ≈ 1 removes the offset.

WEEK 7 · SAT 21 NOV · ROCKETS, ORBITS & SPACE SYSTEMS

Atmosphere, rocket equation, orbits, CubeSats

Play: Launch Pad, Orbit Lab, Planet Hopper · Test: Week 7

Ask while they play

  • "You reached space at 100 km. Why aren't you in orbit?" (no sideways speed)
  • "Why does the one-stage rocket fail even with the biggest tank?"
  • "Which orbit takes longer, 400 km or 20,200 km? Why?"

Quick check answers

  1. v = √(GM ÷ r).
  2. To drop empty tanks and engines; the rocket equation gives diminishing returns.
  3. 10 × 10 × about 11 cm (11.35 cm high).
  4. Data sent from a vehicle to a ground station.

Engineering challenge and Python

Equator: 40,075 km ÷ 23.93 h ≈ 1,675 km/h ≈ 465 m/s. At 60° N: × cos 60° = 837 km/h ≈ 233 m/s. Orbits: 400 km → 7.67 km/s, 92 min; 35,786 km → 3.07 km/s, 1,436 min (23.9 h).

WEEK 8 · SAT 28 NOV · DESIGN REVIEW & SHOWCASE

Present, demonstrate, graduate

Play: Final exam (all weeks) · Showcase Quiz Duel in front of guests

Quick check answers

  1. L = ½ ρ v² S C_L.
  2. x_CG = Σ(m·x) ÷ Σm.
  3. The part can carry twice the design load before it fails.
  4. Proportional, Integral, Derivative.

Showcase idea: put the Quiz Duel on a projector. Guests vote on which game each student demonstrates, and the student explains the physics while they fly.

06TinkerCAD 3D and Codeblocks tasks

Every 3D model in the games is built only from TinkerCAD basic shapes (box, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, wedge). After a game, ask them to rebuild that model in TinkerCAD. In Code Pilot they first practise the logic with game blocks, then repeat it in real Codeblocks.

NoteBlock labels below follow TinkerCAD Codeblocks. If your version shows a slightly different label, use the closest block. Sizes are in mm.

Codeblocks 1 · Quadcopter frame (Week 4)

The trick: two long crossing bars make four arms with no maths. Then rotate the whole group 45° to turn a "+" frame into the "X" frame from the handout.

Create variable ArmLength = 125 Box W: 50 L: 50 H: 4 ← centre plate Box W: 2 × ArmLength L: 10 H: 4 ← arms along X Box W: 10 L: 2 × ArmLength H: 4 ← arms along Y Cylinder radius 14, height 4 → Move X: ArmLength ← motor pad Cylinder radius 14, height 4 → Move X: −ArmLength Cylinder radius 14, height 4 → Move Y: ArmLength Cylinder radius 14, height 4 → Move Y: −ArmLength Cylinder radius 3, height 10, set as HOLE → Move X: ArmLength (repeat for the other 3 pads) Create group (all shapes) Rotate around Z axis: 45°

Parametric challenge: change ArmLength from 125 to 110 and run again. The whole frame updates, just like the SolidWorks global variable in the Week 4 handout. Loop challenge: make the four pads with a Repeat 4 or Count loop.

Codeblocks 2 · Rocket with three fins (Week 7)

Three fins are 360° ÷ 3 = 120° apart. Positions use x = r cos θ and y = r sin θ (polar coordinates, from the Week 4 maths box).

Cylinder radius 10, height 100 ← body Cone radius 10, height 30 → Move Z: 100 ← nose Box W: 15 L: 2 H: 30 → Move X: 17 ← fin at 0° Box W: 15 L: 2 H: 30 → Rotate Z: 120° → Move X: −8.5 Y: 14.7 ← fin at 120° Box W: 15 L: 2 H: 30 → Rotate Z: 240° → Move X: −8.5 Y: −14.7 ← fin at 240° Create group

Check: 17 × cos 120° = −8.5 and 17 × sin 120° ≈ 14.7.

TinkerCAD 3D · 1U CubeSat like PearlAfricaSat-1 (Week 7)

Box 100 × 100 × 113.5 (solid) Box 96 × 96 × 120 (HOLE, centred) → group: a hollow frame Box 80 × 113.5 × 80 (HOLE, centred) → 80 × 80 windows through two sides Box 113.5 × 80 × 80 (HOLE, centred) → windows through the other two 4 × Box 8.5 × 8.5 × 113.5 at the corners (rails) Group everything. Check: outer size 100 × 100 × 113.5 mm.

TinkerCAD 3D · Speed-build aircraft (Week 1)

Fuselage: cylinder turned on its side. Nose: cone or half-sphere. Wing: box 150 mm wide (use the Ruler). Tailplane: smaller box. Fin: wedge. Shortcuts: Duplicate (Ctrl + D), Group (Ctrl + G), Align (L), Mirror (M). Time it, and record the time in the logbook.

07Running the tests

  • Gate Runner (solo): 10 questions, 3 shields. Steer into the gate with the right letter. 9–10 correct = 3 stars. After every gate a pop-up explains the answer, so the test also teaches.
  • Quiz Duel (two players): first correct answer scores, faster answers score more, wrong answers cost 50 points and lock you out of that question. Pilot 1 uses A S D F, Pilot 2 uses J K L ;. On a tablet each player gets their own buttons.
  • Choose one week, several weeks, or Select all for the final exam. The test link at the end of each game's last mission opens that week's test.
  • Each pilot earns XP and stars. Show the hub's pilot cards at the end of the day as a scoreboard.
TipMake the loser of a duel the teacher: they explain one question they got wrong. Explaining is the fastest way to learn.

08If something goes wrong

  • Black screen or "3D engine did not load": connect to the internet once, or keep the lib folder next to the pages (it holds a copy of the 3D library for offline use).
  • Slow on old laptops: close other tabs. The physics adjusts to slow frame rates, so missions still behave correctly.
  • No sound: click anywhere in the game first (browsers only allow sound after a click), and check the speaker icon.
  • Keys don't respond: click once on the 3D view so the page has focus.
  • Wrong pilot got the stars: pick the right pilot on the hub before playing. To start fresh, use Reset all progress at the bottom of the hub (click twice).
  • Stuck on a mission: fail it once: the failure screen gives an engineer's tip. Code Pilot also has a Hint button.
